The abstract is read far more often than the paper, usually on its own: on arXiv, in search results, in databases. So it has to stand alone.

- **One paragraph** that states the problem, what you did, and the main result. Be specific about the result.
- **No citations.** A reader of the abstract cannot follow "Ref. [3]".
- **Define acronyms** in the abstract itself, and again in the main text, because the two are read separately.
- **Length.** Lemma's default profile targets 150–250 words. This is a style choice, not a rule; journals set their own limits, so check your journal's author guide.

A useful test: if someone read only your abstract, would they know what you showed and why it matters?
