Many quantum-information papers collect notation, definitions and known results in a **Preliminaries** (or **Background**) section before the main part. This keeps the main argument uncluttered and gives readers one place to look things up.

Typical contents:

- conventions (e.g. Pauli matrices $X, Y, Z$; logarithms base 2; $[n] = \{1,\dots,n\}$),
- definitions of the objects you use (codes, channels, distance measures),
- prior results you build on, with citations.

**Style choice.** Defining notation where it is first needed, inside the methods, is also fine for shorter papers. What matters is that every symbol is introduced before or where it is used; Lemma's notation sheet collects what your paper defines.
