Appendices hold material that would interrupt the main argument: long proofs, derivations, extra data. Start them with `\appendix`; the following `\section`s are then numbered A, B, …

Two rules keep them useful:

- **The main text points to them** ("the proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}"). Readers do not browse appendices on their own.
- **Every appendix the text mentions exists.** If your journal puts extra material in a separate Supplemental Material file, say so explicitly.

Some journals (e.g. Physical Review Letters) put this material in a separate Supplemental Material file instead of appendices.
