A research paper answers a fixed sequence of questions, whatever its headings are called:

- **Why does this matter?** (introduction: context, the gap, your contribution)
- **What do I need to know first?** (background or preliminaries: notation, prior results)
- **What did you do?** (methods, model, setup or construction)
- **What did you find?** (results: theorems, numerics, data)
- **What does it mean?** (discussion: interpretation, limitations, comparison)
- **So what, and what next?** (conclusion or outlook)

Lemma calls these _roles_. A section titled "Encoding scheme" can fill the methods role; one section can fill several. Papers legitimately differ: a Letter may have no headings at all, and a theory paper may put its main theorem where an experimental paper puts its results.

**Style choice.** The exact headings are not a rule. What matters is that a reader can find each answer. Your journal's author guide may prescribe a structure; check it before you submit.
